# Side-view idle — breathing/weight-shift loop, 2026-10-04 (T-0430)

Second user of the walk's own reference path (`docs/assets/evidence/side-view-walk-reference/`):
hand-cut parts -> post-process key -> deterministic rig and gait, no GPU and no sampling
anywhere. Same ten parts, same `side_view_rig.json` pivots/bone mapping/z-order, reused
unmodified.

Code: `assets/src/character/src/char_gen/idle_cycle.py` (pure motion math) and
`idle_render.py` (the compositor). Generated by `assets/src/character/gen_idle_side_view_T0430.py`,
which is deterministic -- running it twice produces byte-identical output.

## Why idle looks nothing like the walk, mechanically

The walk rotates limbs through keyframed angles every frame. Idle rotates **nothing** --
every part sits at a fixed rest angle (0 degrees) in every frame. Only two things move:

- **Torso + head translate** for breath (vertical) and weight-shift (horizontal, the one
  axis a side view actually has for "lateral or fore-aft").
- **Arms translate** with the torso's full breath offset (the chest carries the shoulder)
  but only a **damped, delayed** copy of its sway -- a trail, not a swing.

The **hip/pelvis point never moves**. Thighs and calves attach directly to it and never
rotate, so the leg chain -- and therefore both feet -- is pixel-identical in every frame.
That is a stronger guarantee than "feet approximately stay near the ground line": it is
*exactly* the same pixels, proven in `tests/test_idle_render.py::TestFeetStayPlanted` by
reading the actual composited alpha channel, not by trusting the rest-angle constant.

## Frame count: 12, not the walk's 8

Idle is slower than the walk and layers two signals instead of driving one gait. Breath
completes 2 cycles per loop and weight-shift 1; 12 frames gives 6 samples per breath cycle
against the walk's reference baseline of 8 samples for its own single-cycle gait, enough
that neither signal's motion between adjacent frames is a large fraction of its own
amplitude.

## Breath and sway: why they don't beat

Breath runs at exactly twice sway's frequency (2 cycles/loop vs. 1) -- a harmonic lock, not
two nearby frequencies drifting in and out of phase. The walk never had to solve this (it
has one gait signal, not two layered ones); T-0430's own edge-case list calls out the
failure mode directly ("if their periods are close the loop will look like a wobble"), so
this is checked in `tests/test_idle_cycle.py::TestBreathAndSwayDoNotBeat`, not just chosen
by eye.

## Measured, not assumed: amplitude survives the descent

The walk's own first attempt used a 20-degree knee that was sub-pixel for most of the
cycle at a 40px figure and read as rigid. The same check, run for idle's two signals,
against the **actually composited and descended** frames (`rig_data_used.json`, written by
the same run that produced the PNGs/GIFs below):

| signal | native amplitude | descended amplitude | measured from composited pixels |
|---|---|---|---|
| breath (peak, curve) | 55.0px | **2.251px** | opaque-centroid Y range: **2.181px** |
| sway (peak, curve) | 35.0px | **1.432px** | opaque-centroid X range: **1.229px** |

The pixel-measured ranges are a little smaller than the curve-peak figures because the
centroid averages over the whole figure, including the legs, which never move and make up
most of the opaque area -- that dilution is expected and is exactly why the curve
amplitudes were sized with a >2x (breath) and >1.4x (sway) margin over the 1px floor
instead of landing right on it. Both numbers clear 1px.

`FIGURE_HEIGHT_NATIVE_PX = 977.54` (head reach 169.28 + spine 231.3 + leg chain 576.96) is
measured from the ten parts' own pixel dimensions and `side_view_rig.json`'s
`attach_torso_local_px`, the same way that file's own `calf_L` correction was measured --
see `idle_cycle.py`'s module docstring for the full derivation.

## Feet: measured, not assumed

`foot_bottom_rows_native_all_frames_identical` and `..._descended_...`: both **True**, for
all 12 frames, both resolutions. Native: `(1117, 1117)`. Descended: `(47, 47)`. The two
sides read identical because `side_view_rig.json`'s own `calf_L` length fix (×1.2929, from
the walk) already equalises both leg chains to within 0.1px -- an existing correction,
reused here exactly as the walk applies it, not a new one.

## Known limitation, carried over from the walk's own parts

Five of the ten parts -- including both `shoulder_R`/`shoulder_L` -- are rectangular crops
rather than traced silhouettes (`parts/side_view/README.md`). Idle does not animate elbow
flexion at all, specifically because of this: a driven bend would be mechanically correct
and still invisible, since the upper arms have no silhouette of their own and read as part
of the coat mass. T-0430's own edge-case list warns against raising amplitude to compensate
for exactly this -- the right response is not adding motion nobody can see, so none was
added. Re-cutting the shoulder parts (already flagged as optional polish on the walk's own
card) would make the existing arm-lag translation read more clearly; it does not require
any change to this animation's code.

## What the next side-view animation can reuse

**Cleanly, with no changes needed:**

- `char_gen.walk_cycle.Curve` / `sample` / `bone_length` -- idle imports these directly;
  walk_cycle.py itself is untouched.
- `side_view_rig.json`'s pivots, bone mapping, z-order, and the `calf_L` length fix --
  idle reads the same file the walk does, no duplication.
- `idle_render._paste_by_pivot` / `_distal` -- generic "place this part so its own pivot
  lands at this anchor" / "find this bone's distal joint at a given angle" helpers. Any
  future rest-pose (not just idle) can reuse both unchanged; a future *animated* pose can
  reuse `_distal` with a nonzero angle (it already takes one) even though idle itself
  never does.

**Needed special handling, specific to idle:**

- The hip-anchor decoupling (legs fixed to an anchor the torso visually moves away from
  for breath/sway) is new to this card -- the walk never needed it, because its own
  vertical bob moves the *whole* figure including the legs, and only one foot needs to be
  near the ground in any given walk frame. An animation that needs the torso to move while
  BOTH feet stay exactly planted (idle's own constraint) will need the same decoupling, not
  a smaller version of the walk's root bob.
- Because every part in idle sits at a fixed rest angle, this card never exercised
  rotation-around-a-pivot end to end for a translating anchor point (only `_distal`'s
  static zero-angle case). A future animation that both translates an anchor **and**
  rotates a part around it (e.g. a lean that also bends a limb) should check that
  combination explicitly -- it is not proven by anything committed here.
